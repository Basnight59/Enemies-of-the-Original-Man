What The Book Argues

A diagnostic assessment
and a legal brief.

Enemies of the Original Man examines the historical, legal, psychological, and theological systems that structured enslavement and colonization — not as past events, but as architectures whose consequences remain active in institutions, identities, and communities of the African diaspora.

Drawing on archival records, case law, psychological literature, and lived experience, the work proposes that the injuries of captivity and domination cannot be understood solely through individual trauma models. They require frameworks capable of addressing intergenerational transmission, institutional normalization, and the moral and legal debts that follow.

The book introduces two interlocking analytical tools — Post-Traumatic Colonization Syndrome and Post-Traumatic Slave Master Syndrome — alongside a proposal for a Diaspora Diagnostic Manual (DDM) as a research and restorative agenda.

This is not a plea for recognition. It is a diagnostic assessment and a legal brief.
Evidentiary Status Note The book distinguishes consistently between documented fact, historical interpretation, and proposed intellectual contribution. That distinction is preserved in site architecture.

Defined Term • /framework/ptcs

Post-Traumatic Colonization Syndrome (PTCS)

PTCS is a proposed analytical framework developed in Enemies of the Original Man for examining the enduring and intergenerational consequences of colonization, captivity, racialized violence, and institutional domination.

PTCS is not presented as a diagnosis recognized by the American Psychiatric Association's DSM. It forms part of a proposed diaspora-centered framework for analysis, research, clinical inquiry, and restorative practice.

Defined Term • /framework/ptsms
2

Post-Traumatic Slave Master Syndrome (PTSMS)

PTSMS is a proposed analytical framework developed in Enemies of the Original Man for examining the enduring psychological, institutional, and cultural effects on populations and systems that constructed, normalized, and inherited the political economy of enslavement.

PTSMS is not presented as a diagnosis recognized by the American Psychiatric Association's DSM. It examines dominant-group adaptation and institutional continuity, not individual pathology alone.

Next: Relationship →
Synthesis • /framework/relationship
3

Relationship Between PTCS + PTSMS

PTCS and PTSMS are designed as interlocking frameworks with diagnostic complementarity. PTCS examines the internalized and transmitted consequences within colonized and diasporic populations; PTSMS examines the institutional and psychological infrastructure of domination.

Together they describe a relational wound — not two isolated conditions, but a single system of injury viewed from its two necessary positions.

Next: DDM →
Proposed Manual • /framework/ddm
4

Diaspora Diagnostic Manual (DDM)

The Diaspora Diagnostic Manual is a proposed research agenda introduced in Enemies of the Original Man for developing diaspora-centered criteria for assessment, clinical inquiry, and restorative practice.

DDM is not the DSM and does not seek DSM recognition as a medical diagnostic manual. It is a proposal for an independent scholarly and community research framework.
